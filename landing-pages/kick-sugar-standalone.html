If you feel like you need sugar or caffeine just to keep your day moving, it may be about more than a sweet tooth.

Maybe you hit the afternoon wall and suddenly coffee, chocolate, or something carby sounds really good.

Maybe you get irritable when you’re hungry.

Maybe you feel shaky if lunch gets pushed too late, feel noticeably better as soon as you eat, or find yourself thinking about something sweet even when you weren’t planning to have it.

Those can all be signs that your blood sugar may be riding a little more of a roller coaster than you’d like.

And once you start trying to smooth out those highs and lows, one of the first things worth looking at is how much added sugar is making its way into your day.

That’s where things can get tricky, because sugar isn’t only hiding in the obvious places. It can show up throughout packaged foods, drinks, snacks, sauces, breakfast foods, and things you may not even think of as particularly sweet.

So simply deciding to “eat less sugar” can leave you wondering what that’s supposed to look like in real life.

That’s where I want to help.

Kick Sugar in 28 gives you a clear, step-by-step reset so you can get a better handle on added sugar, support steadier blood sugar, and build habits you can realistically carry forward after the 28 days are over.

This one’s for you if…

  • You regularly crave sweets, bread, or other carb-heavy foods
  • You rely on coffee or something sweet to get through the afternoon
  • You feel irritable, shaky, foggy, or noticeably worse when you go too long without eating
  • You know there’s probably more added sugar in your diet than you’d like, but you’re not sure where all of it is coming from
  • You’ve tried to “cut back on sugar” before but found yourself right back in the same cravings a few days later
  • You want someone to walk you through what to eat, how to prepare, how to handle cravings, and what to do after the reset is over

You don’t need more willpower. You need a plan that makes changing your habits easier to follow.

Here’s what we’ll walk through together

Kick Sugar in 28 is structured differently from a quick “no sugar challenge.”

Before you start removing things, we’ll spend time setting you up so you know what you’re doing, have the foods you need on hand, and aren’t trying to figure everything out while a craving is staring you in the face.

Then we’ll move through the reset together and finish by talking about how to carry what you’ve learned into a more sustainable, lower-sugar way of eating.

  1. 01

    Set yourself up before you start cutting things out

    The first week is about preparation.

    You’ll begin with a simple Find Your Track assessment so you can choose the version of the program that makes the most sense based on your current eating habits and cravings.

    You’ll also start adding in two foundational habits before the reset gets underway:

    More hydration and a protein-rich breakfast.

    You’ll get a Breakfast Recipe Guide with 15 protein-rich ideas to make that part easier.

    Because I’d much rather help you build something supportive into your day than simply hand you a long list of foods you’re no longer “allowed” to eat.

  2. 02

    Figure out where the sugar is actually coming from

    Next, we’ll take stock of what your current patterns really look like.

    You’ll complete a Starting Assessment and use a Food + Mood Journal throughout the program so you can begin noticing connections between what you eat, how you feel, your cravings, and your energy.

    Then we turn you into a bit of a label detective. You’ll learn how to look beyond the front of the package and spot sources of added sugar that can easily fly under the radar.

    And we’ll go through your pantry together so you can remove some of the foods that make the reset harder and replace them with options that make the next few weeks much easier to navigate.

  3. 03

    Break the sugar-and-craving cycle

    Once you’re prepared, we start the reset.

    This is where you’ll put the food guidelines into practice and begin stepping away from the added sugar and highly processed foods that can keep you riding the spike-crash-crave cycle.

    The program includes guidance for what to eat, what to keep on hand, and what to do when cravings show up, along with two different tracks so the approach isn’t exactly the same for someone with intense cravings and a high-sugar diet as it is for someone who is simply cleaning up a moderate amount of sugar.

    And throughout the process, you’ll keep paying attention to your Food + Mood Journal so you can notice what begins to shift.

    Because the goal isn’t simply to make it through 28 days. It’s to learn something about your own patterns while you’re doing it.

  4. 04

    Build a way of eating you can carry forward

    The reset has an end date. Your healthy habits shouldn’t.

    At the end of the program, we’ll talk about your next steps and how to move forward with a realistic, lower-sugar lifestyle rather than feeling like you have to live in permanent “challenge mode.”

    You’ll have a clearer sense of:

    • where added sugar tends to sneak into your diet
    • what helps you feel more satisfied at meals
    • what your personal cravings tend to look like
    • how your energy and mood respond to your eating patterns
    • and which habits are worth keeping once the 28 days are over

This isn’t about never eating sugar again.

A 28-day reset can give you a really useful opportunity to step away from your usual patterns, notice how you feel, and make some changes with a clear structure around you.

But the bigger goal is to help you become more intentional about how much added sugar is making its way into your everyday diet, and give you habits that make steadier eating feel easier when the program is over.

Because there’s a big difference between choosing to have something sweet and feeling like your energy, cravings, or mood are constantly deciding for you.
